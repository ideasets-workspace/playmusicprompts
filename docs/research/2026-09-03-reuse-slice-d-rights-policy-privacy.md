# Outcome first

1. **Cross-user reuse of Lyria output is permitted by Google's contract.** Google Cloud Service Specific Terms §20(a): "Generated Output is Customer Data. As between Customer and Google, Google does not assert any ownership rights in any new intellectual property created in the Generated Output." [S01, Last modified July 29, 2026, accessed 2026-09-03]. Nothing in §17 or §20 restricts serving Customer Data to other End Users; the only §20 use restrictions are age (d), healthcare (e), the Prohibited Use Policy (c) and the competitive-use/model restrictions in §17. The Lyria 3 documentation page adds, for the preview models the project uses: "Customers may elect to use it for production or commercial purposes, or disclose Generated Output to third-parties" [S06, Last updated 2026-09-02 UTC].
2. **But the project's sung route (`lyria-3-pro-preview`) is a Pre-GA Offering: "not covered by any SLA or Google indemnity", liability capped at the lesser of the Agreement cap or $25,000** [S01 General Service Terms §5(b),(c)]. And **Lyria (any version, including GA `lyria-002`) is absent from the Generative AI Indemnified Services list** (Codey, Gemini, Imagen, PaLM, Veo only; Last modified July 20, 2026) [S03]. So Google's generated-output IP indemnity (§20(i)) does not cover any track this project serves. Cross-user reuse multiplies exposure to any third-party claim on a single track (one generation → N listeners), and the project, not Google, carries it.
3. **Google's indemnity, where it exists, covers only "unmodified" output** [S01 §20(i)(i)]. The project's pipeline masters to −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP (COMMON-BRIEF CONTEXT-03). Even if Lyria were later added to the indemnified list, a mastered copy is arguably "modified"; storing the raw engine WAV alongside the master preserves the option. [INFERENCE from the clause text; not an official ruling.]
4. **Two Google clauses directly shape the reuse design:** §20(b) "a Generative AI Service may, in some scenarios, produce the same or similar Generated Output for multiple customers" (so a "similar catalogue track" is already a contemplated outcome, and uniqueness cannot be promised to users); and §20(d) "Customer will not, and will not allow End Users to, use a Generative AI Service as part of a website ... that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" — a login-free public /musics catalogue must be designed so it is not "likely to be accessed" by minors (age gate or ToS age statement; OWNER-DECISION on mechanism).
5. **SynthID: every Lyria output is watermarked; the Suno-style "do not strip" covenant is not in Google's SST text I read, but the Prohibited Use Policy §4.5 forbids "Misrepresenting the provenance of generated content by claiming it was created solely by a human, in order to deceive"** [S04, Last Modified December 17, 2024]. The mastering chain must be verified not to destroy the watermark (Google claims robustness to MP3 compression, noise, speed change [S07 blog]); there is **no public SynthID audio detection API** — the Detector portal is waitlisted for journalists/researchers [S07, S08 `[single-source official]` for the waitlist restriction]. Lyria 3 also supports C2PA Content Credentials [S06]; preserve them on copy.
6. **Copyright: neither PlayMusicPrompts nor the prompting user owns a copyright in a prompt-only Lyria track in the US** (USCO Part 2, Jan 2025: "prompts do not alone provide sufficient control" [S09 `[FULL]`]), in the EU (Council questionnaire consensus quoted in S09 §III), or in Türkiye (dominant FSEK doctrine: no "hususiyet" in fully AI output [S16, S17, S18]). Consequence: (a) our right to re-serve rests on CONTRACT (Google's SST + our own ToS), not on copyright; (b) users have no copyright to assert against reuse of "their" prompt-only generation, but a **user-written lyric or a detailed creative prompt may itself be a copyrightable literary work** (USCO Part 2 at nn.69–70: "prompts themselves, if sufficiently creative, may be copyrightable"; and "where a human inputs their own copyrightable work and that work is perceptible in the output, they will be the author of at least that portion") — so **sung tracks with user-supplied lyrics need an explicit licence from the user to re-serve**; (c) the ToS must carry a non-exclusive, perpetual licence from the user to PlayMusicPrompts covering prompt, lyrics and output, as Suno's and ElevenLabs' do [S11, S13].
7. **Privacy:** a stored prompt is personal data whenever it identifies a person (GDPR Art. 4(1); KVKK m.3). Storing it privately for service provision and reuse can rest on legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) if the EDPB three-step test is documented [S14 Opinion 28/2024 ¶¶69–73, 92–94]; **publishing raw prompts on a public /musics page is a separate purpose with a different balancing outcome — publish a derived title/description, keep the raw prompt private.** KVKK issued a dedicated generative-AI guide on 24 Nov 2025 [S15] on top of its 2021 recommendations [S15b]; both apply the 6698 principles (lawful basis m.5, minimisation m.4, disclosure m.10, erasure m.7/11). The anonymous `pmp_anon` cookie is an online identifier → personal data under GDPR Recital 30; erasure of a track's prompt must be possible per anonId.
8. **Moderation:** as a hosting service in the EU sense, a shared catalogue needs a DSA Art. 16 notice-and-action mechanism (electronic, substantiated notices; confirmation of receipt; decision with redress info) [S19]; in Türkiye, Law 5651 m.5: the hosting provider "yer sağladığı içeriği kontrol etmek ... ile yükümlü değildir" but must remove unlawful content when notified under m.8/9 and retain traffic data 1–2 years [S20]. User-pasted third-party lyrics are the highest copyright risk in a sung catalogue; the engine's recitation filter reduces but does not eliminate it [S06 "Filtering Input, output (recitation), output (vocal likeness)"].
9. **Disclosure:** AI Act Art. 50 applies from 2 August 2026 [S21 EC FAQ dated 24 July 2026]. As DEPLOYER, PlayMusicPrompts has a human-facing labelling duty only for deepfakes (Art. 50(4)) and public-interest text; instrumental/sung music that does not resemble an existing person is not a deepfake, so the human-facing "AI-generated" label is not mandated by Art. 50(4) — but the PROVIDER-side machine-readable marking (Art. 50(2)) is satisfied upstream by Google's SynthID + C2PA only if the project does not strip them. Google's Prohibited Use Policy §4.5 and the whole-catalogue honesty case make an "AI-generated" label the recommended default anyway; competitors label by watermark/metadata (Suno: "fingerprint, watermark or metadata Suno appends"; ElevenLabs free tier: mandatory "Eleven Music" attribution) [S11, S13].
10. **Recommendation:** cross-user reuse is LAWFUL AND SAFE under five conditions (§Recommendation), the binding ones being: keep the engine's SynthID/C2PA intact on every stored copy; add the user→platform licence and the "your generation may be served to other listeners" disclosure to the ToS before the first reuse; never publish raw prompts; do not reuse sung tracks whose lyrics were user-supplied unless the licence clause is in force; and accept (or get Berk's decision on) the uninsured third-party-IP exposure created by the Pre-GA/non-indemnified status of Lyria.

## Item 2 — Copyright status of AI-generated music and what our ToS must say

### 2.1 United States — USCO Part 2 (January 2025) `[FULL]` [S09]

Five-part depth record (R9, adapted for an official report):
1. **Problem in the authors' framing:** "the copyrightability of outputs generated by AI systems ... the type and level of human contribution sufficient to bring these outputs within the scope of copyright protection" (Exec. Summary).
2. **Method:** doctrinal analysis of >10,000 NOI comments against the human-authorship requirement (Burrow-Giles, Feist, CCNV, Andrien, Thaler), applied to three contribution types: prompts; expressive inputs; modification/arrangement.
3. **Findings (the report has no empirical tables; these are its stated conclusions):** "Based on the functioning of current generally available technology, prompts do not alone provide sufficient control." "Prompts essentially function as instructions that convey unprotectible ideas." "Repeatedly revising prompts does not change this analysis." "The fact that identical prompts can generate multiple different outputs further indicates a lack of human control." "prompts themselves, if sufficiently creative, may be copyrightable. The copyright status of the output generated, however, is a separate question." (n.69–70). "where a human inputs their own copyrightable work and that work is perceptible in the output, they will be the author of at least that portion of the output." Music-specific: UMG's ex parte letter quoted a text-to-music generator warning "[n]o matter how detailed[,] text prompts cannot fully define an actual piece of music" (n.89, citing Udio's guide); the Office notes 170 million AI tracks diluting royalties (UMG) and that AI-only musical works are ineligible for the §115 MLC blanket licence (n.191).
4. **Stated limitations:** "few bright-line rules are possible"; conclusions may be revisited "if further advances in technology provide users with increased control"; Thaler appeal pending at date of report; international approaches "still being formed".
5. **Application here:** (a) PlayMusicPrompts cannot own or register a copyright in a prompt-only track; its exclusivity over the catalogue is contractual and technical only. (b) A listener cannot assert copyright over "their" prompt-only track against cross-user reuse. (c) A listener who types original lyrics (the sung route supports "User-provided lyrics" [S06]) owns the lyrics as a literary work and "at least that portion of the output" in which they are perceptible; **re-serving such a track to another listener is a public performance/distribution of the user's lyrics and needs a licence from the user** — the ToS clause in item 6. (d) Prompt text itself may be copyrightable when "sufficiently creative"; storing and displaying it also needs the same licence.

### 2.2 European Union

- AI Act Art. 50 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): applicability and duties in item 5.
- Copyright Directive (2019/790) context: not researched in the time available → gap.

### 2.3 Türkiye — FSEK

FSEK m.1/B defines an eser as "sahibinin hususiyetini taşıyan ... her nevi fikir ve sanat mahsulleri"; m.8 presupposes a natural-person author. Dominant doctrine (three independent practitioner/academic sources read `[PARTIAL]`): fully autonomous AI output lacks hususiyet and is unprotected/"sahipsiz"; hybrid output may be protected in proportion to human selection/arrangement via m.6 (işlenme) / m.7 (derleme) [S16 Özdağıstanlı Ekici; S17 Emre Kurt 2026-04-26: "Türkiye'de henüz AI odaklı spesifik bir Yargıtay kararı bulunmamakla birlikte"; S18 thesis abstract (Tezara 930365) `[ABS]`: AI music "eser niteliği taşımaması ve yapay zekânın eser sahibi olamaması sonucunu doğurmaktadır"]. No official Turkish guidance (Kültür ve Turizm Bakanlığı / Telif Hakları Genel Müdürlüğü) on AI outputs was found in the searches run; absence not proven (R4.3). Consequence identical to US: reuse rights are contractual; user-written Turkish lyrics are protected literary works.

## Item 3 — Privacy: stored prompts, public display, cookies, consent

**Is a prompt personal data?** Whenever it names or describes an identifiable person ("a song for my wife Ayşe about our Bodrum summer"), yes — GDPR Art. 4(1) / KVKK m.3(1)(d) (definitions quoted in S15b: "Kişisel veri: Kimliği belirli veya belirlenebilir gerçek kişiye ilişkin her türlü bilgiyi"). The `pmp_anon` cookie value is an online identifier and is personal data under GDPR Recital 30 [STATUTE — not re-opened this session, `[UNVERIFIED]` citation form]. Prompts are also logged by Google for abuse review "for up to 90 days" when classifiers flag them [S01 GCP §4 + abuse-monitoring doc, `[PARTIAL]`] — the privacy notice must disclose this sub-processing.

**Lawful basis, private storage (table + reuse index).** EDPB Opinion 28/2024 [S14, adopted 17 Dec 2024, `[PARTIAL]`: executive summary and ¶¶43, 51, 69–73, 92–94 read] applies the GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) three-step test to AI-model deployment: (1) legitimate interest that is "lawful; clearly and precisely articulated; real and present"; ¶69 gives "developing the service of a conversational agent to assist users" as an example; (2) necessity — ¶73: "If the pursuit of the purpose is also possible through an AI model that does not entail processing of personal data, then processing personal data should be considered as not necessary"; (3) balancing, in which "Reasonable expectations play a key role" (¶92). Application: storing the prompt privately to deliver, replay and de-duplicate generations is within a listener's reasonable expectation of a generative player; **the similarity index should be built from the audio embedding and a derived, de-identified brief, not from the raw prompt**, so the reuse path itself processes no personal data (¶73 necessity). KVKK: m.5(2)(f) "ilgili kişinin temel hak ve özgürlüklerine zarar vermemek kaydıyla, veri sorumlusunun meşru menfaatleri" is the KVKK analogue [STATUTE, `[UNVERIFIED]` text not re-opened]; the KVKK 2021 recommendations [S15b, `[PARTIAL]`] address "geliştiriciler, üreticiler, servis sağlayıcılar" and call for privacy-by-design and minimisation; the 24 Nov 2025 "Üretken Yapay Zekâ ve Kişisel Verilerin Korunması Rehberi (15 Soruda)" [S15, announcement page `[FULL]`, guide body not opened] states it evaluates generative-AI processing "6698 sayılı Kişisel Verilerin Korunması Kanunu çerçevesinde" for "veri sorumlusu niteliğini haiz aktörlere".

**Public display (/musics).** Publishing the raw prompt is a new purpose, a disclosure to the public, and fails the ¶92 reasonable-expectations test for a private story typed into a one-prompt box. Recommendation: store `prompt_raw` private; generate and publish `public_title` + `public_description` with a PII scrub; never publish user-supplied lyrics without the explicit "share" action (pending 2026-09-02 order).

**Retention and erasure.** GDPR Art. 5(1)(e), 17; KVKK m.7 (silme/yok etme/anonim hâle getirme) and m.11 rights. Design: erasure by `anonId`/`userId` deletes `prompt_raw`, lyrics and the public title; the audio and its de-identified brief may be kept if genuinely anonymised — EDPB ¶43 sets the anonymity bar high ("insignificant" likelihood of extraction), so a sung track that sings a person's name is NOT anonymised by deleting the prompt; it must be removed from reuse on request.

**Consent Mode / GA4.** Analytics consent (ePrivacy Art. 5(3) / KVKK m.5) is separate from the legitimate-interest basis for service storage; do not bundle them. Reuse-signal telemetry (skip/full-listen) tied to `pmp_anon` is behavioural data — keep it under the same notice, and run the GA4 Consent Mode v2 denied-state without user-level identifiers. [INFERENCE; Consent Mode v2 documentation not opened this session.]

## Item 4 — Content-moderation duty for a shared catalogue

**EU — DSA.** A public catalogue of user-initiated tracks is a "hosting service" (DSA Art. 3(g)(iii)). Art. 16 [S19 text mirror, `[FULL]` for the article]: "(1) Providers of hosting services shall put mechanisms in place to allow any individual or entity to notify them of the presence on their service of specific items of information that the individual or entity considers to be illegal content. Those mechanisms shall be easy to access and user-friendly, and shall allow for the submission of notices exclusively by electronic means." Notices must carry a substantiated explanation, exact electronic location, notifier's name and email, and a good-faith statement; the provider must "send a confirmation of receipt", "notify that individual or entity of its decision ... providing information on the possibilities for redress", and process notices "in a timely, diligent, non-arbitrary and objective manner", disclosing automated means. A qualifying notice gives "actual knowledge" for Art. 6 liability. Plus Art. 17 statement of reasons and Art. 14 terms transparency [not opened; `[UNVERIFIED]`].

**Türkiye — Law 5651 m.5** [S20 Lexpera consolidated text, `[FULL]` for m.5]: "(1) Yer sağlayıcı, yer sağladığı içeriği kontrol etmek veya hukuka aykırı bir faaliyetin söz konusu olup olmadığını araştırmakla yükümlü değildir. (2) Yer sağlayıcı, yer sağladığı hukuka aykırı içeriği bu Kanunun 8 inci ve 9 uncu maddelerine göre haberdar edilmesi hâlinde yayından çıkarmakla yükümlüdür. (3) Yer sağlayıcı, yer sağladığı hizmetlere ilişkin trafik bilgilerini bir yıldan az ve iki yıldan fazla olmamak üzere ... saklamakla ... yükümlüdür." Yer sağlayıcı bildirimi (BTK) obligation not verified this session.

**What beyond the engine's refusal.** The engine filters input, recitation and vocal likeness [S06]; it does not see the public title/description PlayMusicPrompts derives, nor evaluate whether pasted lyrics are a third party's copyrighted text that happens to pass recitation filtering in a new melody. Minimum: (a) text classifier on title/description/lyrics before publication; (b) a lyrics-provenance checkbox ("I wrote these lyrics") with the licence clause; (c) the Art. 16 form; (d) a takedown → unpublish + remove-from-reuse path that also honours §20(i)(3)-style "use after notice" logic even though Google's indemnity does not apply.

## Item 5 — Attribution and disclosure

**AI Act Art. 50** (applies from 2 August 2026 [S21 EC FAQ dated 24 July 2026, `[FULL]`]). Role split: Google is the PROVIDER of the generative system (Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking — satisfied by SynthID + C2PA if preserved; the FAQ: outputs "must be marked with effective, reliable, robust and interoperable machine-readable marks"). PlayMusicPrompts is a DEPLOYER (professional use) and, arguably, also a provider of a downstream AI system placed on the market under its own name ("Ideasets Hybrid Music Composer Model") — `[UNVERIFIED]` legal classification; if provider, Art. 50(2) binds it directly, which again means: do not strip the marks, and consider a detection route (the FAQ says providers must "make available a mechanism" for detection — Google's waitlisted portal may not satisfy this for a downstream provider). Deployer human-facing labels are mandatory only for deepfakes (Art. 50(4): content that "resembles existing persons ... and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic") — a track imitating a real singer's voice would qualify; Lyria's vocal-likeness filter reduces this. A generic AI song is not a deepfake, so **no Art. 50 duty to caption "AI-generated" in the player** — but the Code of Practice icons are available as a recognised label, and Google's PUP §4.5 makes any suggestion of human-only origin a policy violation.

**"Originally generated by another listener."** No law requires it. Honesty and expectation-management favour a neutral label such as "From the PlayMusicPrompts catalogue" rather than exposing the other listener (which would itself be a disclosure of that listener's activity → item 3). OWNER-DECISION on wording.

**How competitors label:** Suno relies on embedded "fingerprint, watermark or metadata" that users may not remove [S11]; ElevenLabs free tier requires visible "Eleven Music" attribution [S13b]; Mubert free tier requires visible attribution as copyright holder [S23]; Google relies on SynthID + C2PA and no visible label [S06, S07].

## Item 6 — Recommendation for this slice, ToS/Privacy clauses, storage split, falsifiers

**Conditions under which cross-user reuse is lawful and safe (all five required):**
1. Provider-terms compliance: keep SynthID/C2PA intact on every stored copy (store the raw engine file; master a separate copy); operate an age statement/gate consistent with SST §20(d); no public claim of uniqueness (§20(b)).
2. User licence in force BEFORE the first reuse: a non-exclusive, perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, sublicensable licence from the listener to PlayMusicPrompts over prompt, lyrics and output, expressly covering serving to other listeners, storage, derived titles and similarity indexing.
3. Privacy: raw prompt and lyrics stored privately; only a scrubbed derived title/description made public; erasure removes the track from the reuse pool; privacy notice discloses Google abuse-logging and the reuse purpose.
4. Moderation: DSA Art. 16 form + Law 5651 takedown path live before the catalogue is public; text classifier on published fields; lyrics-provenance attestation on the sung route.
5. Risk acceptance: Berk explicitly accepts that Lyria output carries no Google IP indemnity and, for `lyria-3-pro-preview`, a $25,000 liability cap and no-notice discontinuation — or decides to route sung generation through a GA model when one exists.

**Draft ToS clauses — DRAFT FOR BERK'S LAWYER, not legal advice (English artifact; Turkish user-facing text is an OWNER-DECISION):**
- *Ownership.* "As between you and PlayMusicPrompts, you own any original text you type (your prompt and any lyrics you wrote). Tracks are generated by an AI system; no copyright may exist in a generated track, and we make no representation that one does."
- *Licence to us.* "You grant PlayMusicPrompts a worldwide, non-exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, sublicensable licence to store, reproduce, adapt, publicly perform, stream and make available your prompt, your lyrics and every track generated from them, including to other listeners of the service, and to create titles, descriptions and similarity indexes from them."
- *Catalogue reuse disclosure.* "To keep playback continuous and affordable, the next track you hear may be a track that was generated earlier for you or for another listener. Such tracks are AI-generated and are not attributed to any individual."
- *Your lyrics.* "By entering lyrics you confirm they are your own or that you hold the rights to license them as above. Do not enter lyrics written by someone else."
- *Age.* "The service is intended for users aged 18 or over." (SST §20(d).)
- *AI disclosure.* "All music on this service is generated by an AI system and carries a machine-readable provenance mark that must not be removed."
- *Privacy policy additions.* Purposes: generation, replay, catalogue reuse, similarity matching, abuse review (incl. Google's up-to-90-day abuse logging); basis: contract + legitimate interest (GDPR 6(1)(b),(f); KVKK m.5(2)(c),(f)); retention; erasure by anonymous ID; public fields = derived title/description only.

**Store privately vs publicly.** Private: `prompt_raw`, `lyrics_raw`, `requestBody`, `anonId`/`userId`, IP/traffic (5651 m.5(3) 1–2 years), engine model id, SynthID-bearing raw audio. Public: derived title, derived description, duration, mood/genre tags, mastered audio (marks preserved), "AI-generated" label. Never public: which listener generated it.

**Falsifiers (which clause, if found, kills cross-user reuse):**
- F1: a Google reading or notice that SST §17(b)(i) "substitute, replace, or circumvent the use of a Google Model" covers replaying stored Customer Data to other end users → reuse is a suspension risk under §20(f). Test: written question to Google Cloud support/account team; until answered, `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- F2: a Lyria-specific Service Term or model card clause limiting Generated Output to the "End User who prompted it" — none found in S01/S05/S06 this session; absence not proven.
- F3: a court or USCO ruling giving prompt-only music users copyright → user consent would become a copyright licence question, not just contract; monitor Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Cir.) and Allen v. Perlmutter.
- F4: a KVKK Board or EDPB decision that catalogue reuse of prompt-derived content is incompatible with the original purpose → would force explicit consent (opt-in) rather than legitimate interest.
- F5: evidence that the mastering chain destroys SynthID detectability → the project would be distributing unmarked AI audio contrary to PUP §4.5 spirit and Art. 50(2); fix the chain before reuse.

# Synthesis — adopt / build / avoid for PlayMusicPrompts

- **ADOPT:** the Customer-Data position under SST §20(a) as the legal root of catalogue rights; SynthID/C2PA preservation as the disclosure backbone; the Suno-style user licence pattern (adapted, without training-use language unless Berk wants it).
- **BUILD:** raw-vs-mastered dual storage; derived-public-fields pipeline with PII scrub; erasure that pulls a track from the reuse pool; DSA Art. 16 form and 5651 takedown flow; lyrics-provenance attestation; age statement.
- **AVOID:** publishing raw prompts; reusing user-lyric sung tracks before the licence clause is live; any UI text implying human authorship or uniqueness; stripping or re-encoding in a way that could damage the watermark; promising "your song is yours" in copyright terms.

# Recommendation for this slice with falsifiers

Cross-user reuse is lawful under Google's terms and under US/EU/TR copyright law (which gives no one a copyright to block it), provided the five conditions in item 6 are met, with F1 (SST §17(b)(i) reading) put to Google in writing and the uninsured-IP exposure put to Berk as an explicit acceptance. One committed recommendation: implement conditions 1–4 as gating tasks of the reuse feature, and ask Berk only the two decisions that are his — (i) acceptance of non-indemnified/preview exposure or a GA-only reuse pool; (ii) user-facing wording of the reuse disclosure and age mechanism.
