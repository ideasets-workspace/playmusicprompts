## Decision served

A GCP-first recommendation can be justified elsewhere by existing integration, operational complexity and dated cost; this academic slice supplies no vendor-performance proof.

## Five-part depth records

### P1 — long-run audio advertising

1. **Problem:** estimate causal listening sensitivity to ad quantity rather than endogenous observed ad exposure.
2. **Mechanism:** randomized persistent 3×3 pod-frequency/pod-length assignment; 34,390,962 observations; 21 months. Equation(1), p16 uses treatment indicators as instruments for realized ads/hour in 2SLS.
3. **Exact evidence:** Table 5 p17 estimates −2.082 normalized listening-hours points per additional ad/hour; Figure 7 p19 shows longer-run sensitivity. Table 9 p31 tests `2β1−β2`: the shorter-pod preference is significant for active days, not total hours.
4. **Authors' limits:** mobile-only manipulation versus all-device outcomes may attenuate effects; spillovers acknowledged (pp37–38); proprietary revenue restrictions prevent full cost-benefit calculation (p33). **Analyst limits:** old US platform/advertising market; neither today's Google fill nor PMP's optimum cadence is identified.
5. **PMP application:** retain randomized cohort assignment, realized ad seconds, no-fill and return-listening metrics; choose a reversible cadence and assess retention, rather than optimize immediate impressions alone. Do not import its ad count as a product default. [P1 author PDF](https://www.davidreiley.com/papers/PandoraListenerDemandCurve.pdf)

### P2 — AsQM

1. **Problem:** combine codec impairment, startup, stall placement and content preference.
2. **Mechanism:** equations(1–2), p2: `AsQM=(QA−ID−IS)×PF`; equations(7–9), pp3–4 fit stall-segment weights by least squares; three content categories and Portuguese-speaking assessors.
3. **Exact evidence:** Table IV p7 defines 53 stall patterns; Figure 8 p8 weights early pauses more heavily; Figure 9 p9 compares predictions. Table VII p9 records perceived resource consumption, not measured watts/CPU.
4. **Authors' limits:** three segments/test-development constraints; further study needed to explain music masking (p9). **Analyst limits:** numerical/category inconsistencies below prevent safe direct implementation; no demonstrated worldwide/device generalization.
5. **PMP application:** log stall position, count and duration alongside startup; assess preference-sensitive cohorts. Use the paper as a measurement-design lead, not a ready-to-deploy QoE formula or proof of negligible battery impact. [P2 author PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.15186)

### P3 — measured Spotify Android behavior

1. **Problem:** characterize native music playback and navigation under constrained networks.
2. **Mechanism:** Android7 Acer tablet, Spotify8.4.88.150, premium account; GUI instrumentation and packet capture;23–27 runs per condition. Measured delays map to Sackl2013's MOS model; no fresh participant MOS.
3. **Exact evidence:** Figure 1 p2 testbed; §4.1 p4 observes whole-track/next-track prefetch and notes wasted-byte/storage tradeoffs. Figures 2–4 pp4–5 separately report startup, stalls and navigation.
4. **Authors' limits:** future packet-loss/jitter measurements and subjective navigation work requested (§5,p6). **Analyst limits:** one old device/app/codec setting, bandwidth limitation not a worldwide CDN head-to-head; thresholds cannot become present-day PMP network guarantees.
5. **PMP application:** measure requested and actually consumed bytes, next-track readiness, startup and browsing separately. Bound prefetch and cancel obsolete queue requests; compare this baseline to segmentation empirically. [P3 publisher](https://doi.org/10.1145/3349611.3355546)

### P4 — browsing delays and music app acceptance

1. **Problem:** separate subsequent audio experience from the whole music application's experience.
2. **Mechanism:** mobile-browser study;55 retained participants,165 tasks; cached media prevents unintended stalls. Randomized search/scroll/playlist delays; Friedman and Mann–Whitney tests with Šidák correction (§§III–IV).
3. **Exact evidence:** Table I /Figure 3 p4: task allocation and separate ratings; Figures 4–5 pp4–5: application ratings/acceptance. Figure 6 p5 compares browsing results with an inherited stalling model, not a new matched stall experiment.
4. **Authors' limits:** future combined and longer delays (§V,p6). **Analyst limits:** mostly 20–29-year-old university contacts, brief instructed sessions; nonsignificance for streaming does not prove universal absence of effect.
5. **PMP application:** keep search/list rendering responsive during playback, and measure both navigation and listening. Test Three.js contention on slow devices rather than assuming audio-only CDN metrics certify the experience. [P4 full text](https://d-nb.info/131043333X/34)

### P5 — parametric quality benchmark

1. **Problem:** compare six regression approaches for planning/monitoring audio quality.
2. **Mechanism:** equation(1), printed 306: `Y=f(x)`; codec, signal class and bitrate plus web startup/stalling. 80/20 split; labels combine POLQA Music with older delay models, not newly collected human MOS.
3. **Exact evidence:** Figures 1–2 printed 306 show inputs; Table 2 printed 309 ranks SVR highest for webcast (PCC 0.9889,RMSE 0.1946); Tables3–4 compare significance; Table 5 measures runtime on i7-7700HQ.
4. **Authors' limits:** low-bitrate codec-specific outliers (printed 308), tuning sensitivity and accuracy/runtime tradeoff. **Analyst limits:** best of 10 reported runs and model-derived targets do not validate a production preference-aware ABR controller.
5. **PMP application:** retain codec/bitrate and actual stall telemetry; validate renditions with listening tests before using estimated quality to automate selection. A larger model is not automatically a better player. [P5 repository](https://dspace.vsb.cz/items/10387b68-d395-490f-9dfd-8b6136be1f62)

## Applicable standards — scoped, not blanket certification

**S1:** RFC8216 §6.2.1 requires atomically visible playlist changes and immediate availability of listed segments; §6.2.4 requires matching content/timestamps/discontinuity sequences across variants. §8.4 includes audio-only rendition; §10 identifies URI/parser threats and securing key delivery with TLS plus a protected realm/session token. §6.3.6 gives the client decryption-key use. This supports a portable audio rendition contract, not protection against authorized-client capture. [RFC8216](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/inline-errata/rfc8216.html)

**S2:** P.1203.2 is a short-term audio-quality module within audiovisual streaming quality estimation, not a complete personalized music-listening model. Its official scope states one-second outputs feed later integration with startup/stalls. Do not label a raw audio-module output an end-to-end PMP satisfaction score. [ITU official scope](https://www.itu.int/itu-t/recommendations/rec.aspx?lang=en&rec=13401)

Analyst security application: public listening means a client receives useful media. Separate the official master-download entitlement from ordinary listening-rendition access. Protect origin objects, token lifetime and key/URL logging. This slice does not establish browser rewarded-event authenticity; Google's actual SDK/SSV/publisher policies belong to the ads slice. No claim that DRM, signed URLs or HLS make music unrecordable.

For an initial personalized queue, these gates can be tested with owned immutable track objects and a conventional player. Adaptive packaging remains an option to validate; a live synchronized-radio pipeline is a separate owner decision.
