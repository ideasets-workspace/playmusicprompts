# Five-part academic depth records (R9)

## A-01 Brost, Mehrotra, Jehan (Spotify Research), The Music Streaming Sessions Dataset, WWW'19 — [FULL]
1. Problem (authors' framing): "there are no public datasets currently available" to model how users interact with streamed content; two central challenges — predicting (1) skips and (2) context switches (§1).
2. Method: release of 160 M sessions (10–20 tracks, ≤60 s inactivity gap, 8-week uniform sample from radio, personalised mixes, own collections, top-100 playlists); per-row schema in Table 1 (skip_1 "played very briefly", skip_2 "played briefly", skip_3 "most of the track was played", not_skipped, context_switch, no/short/long pause, num_seekfwd/bk, shuffle, hour_of_day, premium, context_type, reason_start, reason_end, uniform_random); ~3.7 M tracks with audio features (Table 2: acousticness, beat strength, bounciness, danceability, dyn range mean, energy, flatness, instrumentalness, key, liveness, loudness, mechanism, mode, organism, speechiness, tempo, time signature, valence, acoustic vector); a uniformly-random-shuffle subset with playlist snapshots for counterfactual evaluation (§3.1).
3. Numbers: non-skip rate "relatively constant … between 34% and 35% for all session positions" (§4.2, Fig. 3); skip rates higher for longer sessions; reason_start most associated with not skipping = previous track played to completion (Fig. 4); context switches more likely at session start (Fig. 6); sessions of length 20 over-represented by the cap (Fig. 1).
4. Limitations (authors): skip times bucketed, not precise; sessions capped at 20 for privacy; popularity threshold excludes rare tracks; commercial/privacy compromises (§6). Analyst: 2018 data, Spotify-only, catalogue tracks — transfer to generated takes unproven.
5. Application to PMP: (a) instrument listening_session.dart with the same skip taxonomy (very brief / brief / mostly / complete) rather than the current binary <10 s reject vs ≥90 % exploit, so the queue policy has graded feedback; (b) log reason_start/reason_end per take for the T−90 s generation trigger evaluation; (c) the authors' own closing sentence — predicting "moments in the track where skips are most likely … for generative and interactive music models" — is exactly PMP's opportunity: emit per-take skip-hazard curves and let the player pre-empt with a variation before the hazard peak.

## A-02 Haruvi et al. (Arctop), Measuring and Modeling the Effect of Audio on Human Focus…, Front. Comput. Neurosci. 2022 — [FULL]
1. Problem: which physical properties of audio increase focus; prior work low temporal resolution (one label per song).
2. Method: N=62 recruited (51 analysed after exclusions: 3 headband misplacement, 2 instruction failures, 6 internet), 4 at-home 1-h sessions, Muse-S 4-channel EEG at 256 Hz, Neuos SDK decoding at 5 Hz, per-participant random-forest calibrated on short tasks then applied to a 30-min Preferred Task; conditions: Spotify "Focus Flow", Apple Music "Pure Focus" (downloaded Sept 2020), Endel "Focus" soundscape on the participant's own device, silence; repeated-measures ANOVA + Holm-Bonferroni; audio-feature model: pyAudioAnalysis 136 properties in 30-s windows → PCA (33 comps, 95 % var) → linear regression with backward elimination, 18 songs, 14/4 split.
3. Numbers: brain-decoded focus vs self-report AUC 0.83 (SD 0.19), r(414)=0.6; Endel vs silence M=0.090, SE=0.027, t(50)=−3.38, p=0.008; Apple p=0.11, Spotify p=0.65 (n.s.); working subgroup Endel vs silence p=0.017; age<36 all audio > silence p=0.004/0.001/0.005; time series: Endel > silence 87 % of the 30 min from 2.5 min; Apple > silence 60 % from 12.5 min; Spotify > silence 27 % from 17.5 min; Endel > Spotify 37 % (6–17 min); Endel–Apple 0 %; audio-only model r=0.72 validation, 88 % accuracy, AUC 0.91; best predicted genres classical, engineered soundscapes, natural sounds; worst pop, hip-hop (Fig. 10).
4. Limitations (authors): could not disentangle personalisation (no pre-recorded soundscape control); no self-selected playlist or skip-allowed condition; female subgroup underpowered; task order not randomised; large inter-participant variance; "seamlessness … played continuously without gaps … was also critical part of the observed effect" (Discussion). Analyst: all six authors are Arctop Inc. employees (vendor of the decoding SDK) and Endel is a commercial partner — conflict of interest; soundscape raw audio was not analysable.
5. Application to PMP: the gapless-continuity confound is the design requirement — PMP's take-to-take boundary must be audibly continuous (crossfade or generated transition), otherwise the generative advantage measured here is forfeited; the "focus within 2.5 min" curve argues for the first take to be ready before the user's intent decays (aligns with buffer ≥2 rule); audio-feature→focus scoring is a candidate "why this take" signal for the Now Playing surface.

## A-03 Seshadri (Georgia Tech), Knees (TU Wien), Leveraging Negative Signals with Self-Attention for Sequential Music Recommendation, RecSys'23 MuRS workshop — [FULL]
1. Problem: sequential recommenders rarely incorporate negative session-level feedback (skips); music is short-form and cold-start heavy.
2. Method: SASRec-like unidirectional and BERT4Rec-like bidirectional transformers (2 encoder blocks, 8 heads, d=128, sessions padded to 20); sampled softmax with 1,000 negatives; skip-informed InfoNCE contrastive loss L = α·L_NCE + β·L_NLL with α=β=0.5, cosine scoring, positive = next non-skipped track p_m, negatives = skipped tracks (skip strength 1–2); MSSD subset ~450 K sessions, ~2 M interactions, ~1 M tracks, ~15 % skipped; HR@K with 1,000 sampled negatives.
3. Numbers (Table 1): Unidir HR@1 .2821 → .3073 (+8.93 %) with c=e_ti; HR@5 .4803 → .5088 (+5.93 %); HR@10 .5593 → .5867 (+4.90 %); HR@20 .6316 → .6554 (+3.77 %); Bidir HR@1 .1945 → .2339 (+20.26 %); Bidir HR@20 with c=e_ti −1.30 % (the one regression).
4. Limitations (authors): no long-term user profiles (pseudonymised), no content/context features, gains wane as K grows, bidirectional under-performs (short dense sequences); future work: per-session-type analysis. Analyst: single dataset, 1,000-negative sampled evaluation inflates HR; workshop paper (peer-reviewed workshop, not main track).
5. Application to PMP: PMP's "early skip <10 s reject" is a strength-1 skip; the paper shows the value of treating skipped takes as explicit negatives when choosing the next prepared take's prompt parameters — a contrastive term over the 101 request params is the direct analogue; top-rank effect (HR@1 +9–20 %) matters most because PMP presents exactly one next take.

## A-04 Meggetto, Revie, Levine, Moshfeghi, Why People Skip Music? … DRL, CHIIR'23 — [PARTIAL: abstract, §1–4.4 read via grep; results tables not read]

2. Method: Vanilla DQN (3×128 FC, replay 10,000, batch 256, lr 0.001, γ 0.9, Tensorforce) framed as classifier on MSSD second-half-of-session skip_2 prediction; SHAP + ablation over user-behaviour (UB), context (CX), content (CN) feature groups; baselines = WSDM Cup 2019 top submissions (Multi-task RNN, Multi-RNN, Temporal Meta-learning, Weighted RNN).
3. Numbers: paper reports "significant improvements over all baselines on both MAA and FPA" — exact values NOT read (tables not in grep output) — [UNVERIFIED].
4. Limitations (authors): temporal data leakage in MSSD features session_length and reason_end (future information) that "inadvertently affected the Spotify Challenge and thus the baselines"; comparison reported without removal for fairness.
5. Application to PMP: never feed reason_end or session-length-to-date into a live next-take policy; behaviour features dominate content features → PMP's skip/complete telemetry outranks audio-feature inputs for queue decisions.
