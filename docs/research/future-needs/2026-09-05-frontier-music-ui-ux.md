# Outcome first

1. **The one hard, dated engineering constraint is 2 December 2026.** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI, OJ L 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026) adds Article 111(4) to the AI Act: providers of generative AI systems "generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, that have been placed on the market before 2 August 2026 shall take the necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026" [S02 FULL, line 304]. Art. 50 itself applies from 2 August 2026 [S01 FULL; S04 quick-facts]. PlayMusicPrompts' generative engine is a *provider* system already on the market → machine-readable marking + a detection mechanism must exist on every generated track by 2026-12-02. This is an engineering item for the audio pipeline, but it also fixes an interface requirement: every player/track surface must be able to *show* provenance (Content Credentials) and the Studio must never strip it on export.
2. **Deployer labelling (Art. 50(4)) is narrower than the industry assumes:** it attaches to *deep fakes* (content resembling existing persons, places, events) and to public-interest text; ordinary generated music is not a deep fake unless it imitates a real artist [S01 FULL; S03 secondary]. The Code of Practice (published 10 June 2026; ~190 signatories by end-July 2026) [S01 FULL] nevertheless specifies the modality-specific form a label takes when it IS owed: for audio, "short audible disclaimer in plain language at the beginning", reminders at regular intervals for long-form/live content, and "additional visual disclosure where a screen is available" [S03 secondary, quoting CoP sub-measure 1.2.3 — `[single-source secondary]` until the CoP PDF is opened]. The three EU icons ("AI", "AI GENERATED", "AI MODIFIED"; SVG/PNG, four variants) are optional; the disclosure duty is not [S05 FULL].
3. **Provenance standard is settled: C2PA Content Credentials 2.4 (April 2026)** natively covers MP3 (ID3 GEOB), WAV/BWF (RIFF chunk), M4A/MP4 (BMFF uuid box), and, new in 2.4, OGG Vorbis (dedicated logical bitstream) [S06 FULL spec lines 471, 5146–5152; S07 secondary]. Soft bindings (fingerprint or invisible watermark, `c2pa.soft-binding`, `c2pa.watermarked.bound` action) let a stripped manifest be recovered from a manifest repository — a "Durable Content Credential" [S06 lines 215–235, 1246–1261, 5715–5750]. Suno already ships C2PA on downloads and exposes a free detection API returning `verified_suno | no_suno_provenance | inconclusive` [S13 FULL]. Design consequence: a **Content Credentials affordance on every track page and player** (web + Flutter), and an export path that preserves the ID3/RIFF/BMFF box.
4. **Store policy is now explicit about generative content (both stores, dated).** Google Play Developer Program Policy effective 26 August 2026 requires apps that generate content with AI to prevent Restricted Content and to "contain in-app user reporting or flagging features that allow users to report or flag offensive content to developers without needing to exit the app" [S08 FULL, lines 560–570]. Apple App Review Guidelines last updated 8 June 2026: 5.1.2(i) "clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties" including third-party AI, with explicit permission; 4.5.3 bans spam via Live Activities; 2.5.18 confines display ads to the main binary and requires age-appropriate ads [S09 FULL, lines 180, 330, 440–441, 479]. Design consequence: an in-player **Report** control on every generated track (mobile), and a contextual consent moment when a prompt leaves the device to a third-party model.
5. **Accessibility law is in force and the yardstick is about to move.** EAA (Directive 2019/882) has applied since 28 June 2025 to covered services including mobile apps; the operative harmonised standard is still EN 301 549 V3.2.1 (WCAG 2.1 AA) because V4.1.1 (WCAG 2.2 AA, ETSI adopted 24 Aug 2026, published Sept 2026) is not yet cited in the Official Journal [S10 secondary; S11 secondary; S12 secondary — three independent secondary sources agree; primary ETSI text not opened → `[UNVERIFIED primary]`]. Design consequence: build to WCAG 2.2 AA now (24×24 px targets, focus appearance, dragging alternatives) so the OJ citation is a no-op.
6. **Form-factor curves remove two escape hatches.** Android 16 (API 36): orientation, resizability and aspect-ratio restrictions are ignored on displays with smallest width ≥ 600 dp; edge-to-edge opt-out is deprecated and disabled; predictive back is on by default [S14 FULL, lines 15–26, 62–99]; Google's blog states API 37 removes the opt-out entirely [S15 FULL]. Apple: Liquid Glass adapts automatically for standard components; custom controls must be tested under Reduce Transparency / Reduce Motion; `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` exists as the opt-out for builds against previous SDKs [S16 FULL, lines 51, 296, 428]; secondary reports say the key is ignored when building with Xcode 27 [S17, S18 secondary — `[UNVERIFIED primary]`]. Design consequence: the tablet Player/Queue/Studio must be adaptive in both orientations and any aspect ratio; glass chrome must have a designed opaque fallback.
7. **Audio-watermark technology is mature enough to be a product requirement, not a research item.** AudioSeal (Meta, ICML 2024) reports detection accuracy 1.00 after MP3 and AAC compression, 0.99 at 1.25× speed-up, average 0.96 across 15 edits vs 0.85 for WavMark, and two-orders-of-magnitude faster detection [S19 FULL, Table 3, Fig. 6]. Google's Lyria outputs carry SynthID by default per third-party reporting [S20 secondary — `[UNVERIFIED primary]`; Google's own SynthID page not opened this session].

## F1 — EU AI Act Article 50 (IN-FORCE from 2026-08-02; 50(2) transitional to 2026-12-02)

- Authority: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Art. 50, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 [S02 FULL]. Applicability: Art. 50 applies from 2 August 2026 [S01 FULL; S04]. New Art. 111(4): providers of generative AI systems "placed on the market before 2 August 2026 shall take the necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026" [S02, line 304]; recital 38 explains the "transitional period of four months" [S02, line 152]. Art. 50(7) was replaced so that the Commission "shall encourage and facilitate the drawing up of codes of practice" and assess their adequacy taking utmost account of the Board [S02, line 247]; the Commission and the Board have confirmed the June 2026 Code as adequate [S01 FULL].
- Duty holders: **provider** — 50(2) machine-readable marking + detectability (PlayMusicPrompts' engine); **deployer** — 50(4) disclosure of deep fakes and public-interest text; 50(5) horizontal: clear, distinguishable, at the latest at first exposure, accessible [S01; S03 FULL; S05].
- Penalty: not re-read this session (Art. 99 not opened) → `[UNVERIFIED]`; the prior in-repo slice-g doc states 2026-08-02 applicability consistently (C16) [in-repo, not re-verified beyond the dates above].
- What the CoP asks of audio when a label IS owed (deep fake): audible plain-language disclaimer at the beginning; reminders at regular intervals for long-form/live; additional visual disclosure where a screen exists [S03 FULL, quoting CoP sub-measure 1.2.3 — `[single-source secondary]`]. Provider marking: layered metadata + imperceptible watermark + fingerprint/logging fallback, synchronised across modalities [S03; Weil summary via Q1 card].
- **Application to PlayMusicPrompts:** (a) engineering: C2PA manifest + robust watermark on every generated track by 2026-12-02 (Outcome 1); (b) interface: a persistent, non-intrusive "Made with AI · Content Credentials" affordance on `/t/<id>`, on the Player and on the Studio export sheet, using the EU "AI GENERATED" icon or an equivalent label; (c) the real-time generative player is *live content* — if any generated segment ever imitates a real artist or place, the CoP's "audible disclaimer at the beginning + reminders after interruptions" pattern applies, and the ad-break boundary is the natural "interruption" at which a short audible reminder can be scheduled without harming the listening flow. OWNER-DECISION: whether to voluntarily label *all* generated music (not legally owed) as a trust feature.

## F2 — C2PA Content Credentials 2.4 (STANDARD, current)

- Version 2.4, April 2026: "Added support for OGG Vorbis audio files", "Added some new actions to better support audio assets", "Introduced fine-grained watermarking actions" [S06 lines 471–493]. Ogg handling: each logical bitstream is a hashed 'box' keyed by `bitstream_serial_number` [S06 lines 5146–5152]. Soft bindings: fingerprint or invisible watermark; a "Durable Content Credential" is one whose soft binding enables manifest recovery from a repository [S06 lines 215–235, 1246–1257]; `c2pa.soft-binding` assertion schema with `alg`, `alg-params`, `bindingMetadata`, `value` [S06 lines 5725–5750]; `c2pa.watermarked.bound` vs `.unbound` roles [S06 lines 3963–3965]. Container methods: WAV/BWF RIFF chunk; MP3 ID3v2 GEOB; M4A/MP4 BMFF uuid box; FLAC via ID3v2-compatible path; Ogg dedicated bitstream [S07 FULL — secondary matrix, consistent with S06].

## Five-part academic and technical records

### S19 — AudioSeal (San Roman et al., Meta FAIR, ICML 2024, arXiv 2401.17264v2) [FULL]

1. **Problem (authors' framing):** existing audio watermarks were designed for data hiding, "not adapted for detection"; decoders "were never trained on non-watermarked samples", giving "poor or overestimated detection rates"; passive deepfake classifiers generalise badly to new generators [S19 lines 29–33].
2. **Method:** jointly train a generator that predicts an additive watermark waveform δ from input audio and a detector that outputs the probability of watermark presence *per sample*; training augmentations include bandpass, boost/duck, echo, high/low-pass, pink/Gaussian noise, speed change, smoothing, resampling, MP3/AAC/EnCodec compression [line 99]; a perceptual (TF-loudness) loss replaces SI-SNR minimisation [line 164]; optional 16-bit message for attribution across N model versions [line 245].
3. **Numbers:** Table 3 (10k ten-second VoxPopuli clips, balanced): accuracy 1.00 for None/Bandpass/Boost/Duck/Echo/Pink/Resample/AAC/MP3, 0.99 Lowpass/Fast 1.25×/Smooth, 0.98 EnCodec, 0.91 White noise, 0.61 Highpass; average 0.96 (TPR 0.98 / FPR 0.04) vs WavMark 0.85 (0.85/0.14). Localisation IoU 0.99 at one second of manipulated speech vs WavMark 0.35 (Fig. 5). Runtime: one order of magnitude faster generation, two orders faster detection than WavMark (Fig. 6, Table 5). Attribution at FPR 1e-3 over 100 message versions (Table 4).
4. **Limitations:** stated — high-pass filtering is the weak edit; evaluation is speech-centric (VoxPopuli), music appears only in appendix Tables 7–8 (mixed speech+music, MusicGen); adversarial gradient attacks degrade detection when the attacker has the detector [§6, line 284]. Analyst — no evaluation of the ID3/RIFF metadata layer, so C2PA + AudioSeal interoperability is an integration claim, not a measured one; music-only robustness numbers are not in the main tables.
5. **Application here:** sets the acceptance test for PlayMusicPrompts' Art. 50(2) marking: detection ≥ 0.99 after MP3/AAC at FPR ≤ 1e-3 on our own generated music, measured before 2026-12-02; the credential sheet's "watermark verified" state is shown only when the detector fires.

# Synthesis — adopt / build / avoid

- **ADOPT:** C2PA 2.4 Content Credentials as the provenance container for every export (MP3 GEOB / WAV RIFF / M4A BMFF / Ogg bitstream) [S06]; the EU "AI GENERATED" icon family as the visible label vocabulary [S05]; WCAG 2.2 AA as the accessibility target for web and Flutter [S10–S12]; Android window-size-class adaptive layouts and predictive back [S14]; Liquid Glass standard components on iOS with tested custom fallbacks [S16].
- **BUILD:** (1) durable watermark soft binding with an acceptance test modelled on AudioSeal Table 3 [S19]; (2) a "Content Credentials" sheet in Player and on `/t/<id>` distinguishing metadata-present from watermark-verified; (3) an in-app Report/Flag control on every generated track (Play policy) [S08]; (4) a contextual third-party-AI consent moment (Apple 5.1.2(i)) [S09]; (5) an audible-disclaimer scheduler keyed to ad-break boundaries for any future deep-fake-class content [S03]; (6) a public `/credentials` verifier page (trust + GEO).
- **AVOID:** fixed-orientation tablet layouts and pillarboxing (API 36/37) [S14, S15]; glass chrome without an opaque fallback [S16]; promotional Live Activities or ads outside the main binary [S09 4.5.3, 2.5.18]; export paths that re-encode and strip ID3/RIFF chunks [S06, S07]; treating the Omnibus as a general 2027 delay (Contradiction 1).

# Committed recommendation and alternatives / tradeoffs / falsifiers

**Recommendation:** treat **2026-12-02** as the design-freeze date for the provenance/labelling layer and make "Content Credentials visible on every surface + Report on every track + adaptive in every orientation + WCAG 2.2 AA + glass-with-fallback" the five non-negotiable constraints that all three design directions must pass before Berk sees them. Alternative: label *nothing* beyond the legal minimum (deep fakes only) — legally defensible per CE-03, but it forfeits the trust/GEO artefact and leaves the product exposed if a later delegated act broadens 50(4). Falsifiers: an OJ act moving the 50(2) date; Apple/Google adding an explicit AI-badge rule (would convert the OWNER-DECISION in F1 into a duty).

### GAP-1 · DSA Article 26 — online-advertising transparency (S20, [FULL] for the cited articles)

Article text as served by EUR-Lex (capture lines 940–962), quoted verbatim: *"1. Providers of online platforms that present advertisements on their online interfaces shall ensure that, for each specific advertisement presented to each individual recipient, the recipients of the service are able to identify, in a clear, concise and unambiguous manner and in real time, the following: (a) that the information is an advertisement, including through prominent markings, which might follow standards pursuant to Article 44; (b) the natural or legal person on whose behalf the advertisement is presented; (c) the natural or legal person who paid for the advertisement if that person is different from the natural or legal person referred to in point (b); (d) meaningful information directly and easily accessible from the advertisement about the main parameters used to determine the recipient to whom the advertisement is presented and, where applicable, about how to change those parameters. 2. Providers of online platforms shall provide recipients of the service with a functionality to declare whether the content they provide is or contains commercial communications. … 3. Providers of online platforms shall not present advertisements to recipients of the service based on profiling as defined in Article 4, point (4), of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 using special categories of personal data referred to in Article 9(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679."* Article 28(2) (line 963 ff.): *"Providers of online platform shall not present advertisements on their interface based on profiling … using personal data of the recipient of the service when they are aware with reasonable certainty that the recipient of the service is a minor."* Article 28(3): compliance *"shall not oblige providers of online platforms to process additional personal data in order to assess whether the recipient of the service is a minor."*

Who is bound. The duty holder in every paragraph is the **provider of an online platform**. Article 3 (line 580) defines an online platform as *"a hosting service that, at the request of a recipient of the service, stores and disseminates information to the public"*; Article 3 (line 598) defines advertisement as information *"presented by an online platform on its online interface against remuneration specifically for promoting that information"*; 'online interface' (line 588) expressly includes *"applications, including mobile applications"*. PlayMusicPrompts stores user-prompted tracks and disseminates 33 of them to the public on `/t/<id>` pages (measured in the growth companion), so the hosting-plus-public-dissemination test is met on its face; whether that activity is "a minor and purely ancillary feature" of a generation service is a legal-characterisation question for Berk's counsel, not for this worker — recorded as `[UNVERIFIED legal characterisation]`. If PMP is an online platform, Article 26 binds **PMP** as the presenter of the ad on its own interface, not Google as the ad intermediary; Google's role as the SDK/network supplies the per-ad "why this ad" data and advertiser identity that PMP must surface, but the obligation to make (a)–(d) identifiable "in real time, for each specific advertisement" sits on the interface owner. Article 19 (line 811, "Exclusion for micro and small enterprises") removes Section 3 — which contains Articles 26–28 — for providers that qualify as micro or small enterprises under Recommendation 2003/361/EC, unless designated a VLOP; PMP's enterprise size is a fact only Berk holds → `[OWNER-FACT NEEDED]`. Article 39 (line 1182) adds an ad repository only for VLOPs/VLOSEs — not applicable.

Design consequence for items 8 and 11 of the owner's list: every audio ad break and every banner in the rebuilt phone, tablet and web surfaces must carry (i) a visible "Ad" marking during the ad, (ii) a one-tap disclosure surface naming the advertiser and payer and explaining the main targeting parameters with a way to change them, and (iii) no profiling-based ads where age is known with reasonable certainty to be under 18 — which, combined with the TR/EEA opt-in consent model already decided, argues for non-personalised ads as the default state and a disclosure component designed as a first-class UI element rather than a Google-rendered afterthought. This is a design constraint on the three directions, not a decision this worker makes.

### GAP-2 · visionOS and Android XR — what a music player must do to be eligible (S29 [FULL], S30 [FULL]; Apple HIG page [PARTIAL])

Android XR (S29): the UI system has two spatial modes — **Home Space** (multiple apps side by side, 2D panels) and **Full Space** (one app, fully immersive). Apps are composed from **spatial panels** and **orbiters** (floating controls anchored to a panel, e.g. transport controls); the guide's floors are comfort and ergonomics (content inside the comfortable field of view, no sustained upward or downward gaze), spatial elevation of interactive elements, and input via eyes+hands with a minimum target-size treatment so hand-ray/gaze selection is reliable. A music player is eligible in Home Space as an existing large-screen (adaptive) Android app with no code change; a spatialised player adds an orbiter for transport and, optionally, a Full Space for the visualiser. Apple visionOS (S30): apps are built from **windows** (2D SwiftUI scenes), **volumes** (bounded 3D) and **immersive spaces**; **ornaments** attach controls to a window edge (Apple's own Music app uses one for transport); spatial audio is default through AVFAudio/PHASE; input floors are indirect eye-gaze + pinch, with hover effects required on interactive elements. Eligibility for an iPad-class Flutter app: iPad apps run on visionOS in a compatibility window; a native visionOS target is a separate SwiftUI/RealityKit surface. **Flutter caveat (this-session reading of the APIs slice, Q30): the companion states no official WebGPU/compute path exists in Flutter; the same slice does not record an official Flutter visionOS target — so any visionOS/Android XR native surface is a future-needs watch item, not a rebuild deliverable.** Claim class: both platform pages are first-party; cross-verification is by two independent vendors describing the same primitives (windows/panels, ornaments/orbiters, gaze+hand input).
