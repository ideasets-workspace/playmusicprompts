# Outcome first

1. **The strongest measured growth lever available to an interface is a passive, automatic broadcast artefact — not a "share" button.** Aral & Walker's randomized field experiment on Facebook (9,687 users; 405 control, 4,600 passive-broadcast, 4,682 active-personalised; 1.4 million peers; 44 days) found that passive-broadcast viral features raised peer adoption by **246%** over the no-viral baseline, while adding active-personalised invites added only a further **98%** (344% total); per message, one invite raised peer adoption 6% vs 2% per notification, but notifications were sent ~70,000 times vs 160 invites, so broadcast won on total adoption [S01 FULL, §4.1, §5.1, Table 4, Table 5]. **Design consequence:** every listen, every generated track and every queue should *automatically* produce a public, indexable, embeddable artefact (a `/t/<id>` page with real HTML, OG image, oEmbed) so that sharing costs the user nothing; the manual share sheet is the second, higher-intensity lever.
2. **Playlists are causal growth machines with published magnitudes.** Aguiar & Waldfogel (NBER w24713 / J. Ind. Econ. 2021): being added to Spotify's Today's Top Hits (18.5 M followers) raised daily streams by 3.346 normalised streams (s.e. 0.28); removal cut 2.757 (0.09); over an average 74.4-day spell that is ≈ 19.4 M additional streams, worth US$116,397–162,956 at US$6–8.4/1,000 streams; ≈ 259,531 daily streams per listed song, i.e. ~10% of the mechanical maximum; New Music Friday #1 placement put ~85% of songs on the national top-200 [S02 FULL, §4.1–4.2, §5, Tables 2–4]. **Design consequence:** editorial and algorithmic *public playlists* (`MusicPlaylist` pages) are a first-class surface on web and in the apps, not a library feature.
3. **Generative-engine visibility is earned by content shape, not keywords.** Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024, GEO-bench 10 k queries): adding quotations (+27.8 → visibility 27.2–27.8 vs baseline ~19–20 on Position-Adjusted Word Count), statistics (+25.9), and cited sources (+24.9) each lift generative-engine visibility by roughly 30–40%; fluency alone +15–30%; keyword stuffing ≈ 0; the effect is largest for pages ranked lower in classic search (Cite Sources: +115.1% for rank-5 sources) [S03 FULL, Table 1, Table 2]. Chen et al. (arXiv 2509.08919, Sept 2025) measured on 1,000 consumer prompts × 2 regions that AI search cites **Earned** media 72.7–92.1% of the time vs Brand 22–27% and Social ≈ 0%, whereas Google's mix was 31.8–54.1% Earned, 22.8–53.8% Brand, 10.9–23.1% Social; top-5 domain overlap between Google and AI search was only 15–32% [S04 FULL, §Results]. **Design consequence:** `/t/<id>` and `/playlist/<id>` pages must carry quotable, statistical, cited text in the HTML (prompt, duration, BPM, key, generation model, Content Credential status), and the product needs *earned* third-party pages to be cited at all.
4. **llms.txt is agent-readiness, not citation lift — the evidence is consistent across five independent measurements.** Google Search Central (June 2026 doc) states AI Overviews/AI Mode have "no additional technical requirements" beyond indexing and snippet eligibility [S05 FULL]; SE Ranking (~300,000 domains) found no correlation and removing the variable *improved* the citation model [S06 FULL, S07]; Ahrefs found 97% of 137,210 llms.txt files were never fetched in May 2026 [S07]; MaxAEO (2,400 domains, 326 adopters, 8 engines, 90 days) found no measurable lift [S08]; Crawlmind (top-10k crawl, 4.2% adoption) reports customer-interview lift only for the `/llms-full.txt` companion and only on ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude, not Google [S09]. **Design consequence:** ship `/llms.txt` + `/llms-full.txt` cheaply for coding-agent and agentic-browser readability (Lighthouse 13.3 audit, May 2026 [S07]), but spend the SEO/GEO budget on structured HTML, schema.org and earned mentions.
5. **The embed contract to copy is Spotify's oEmbed:** `GET https://open.spotify.com/oembed?url=…` returns `type: "rich"`, `html` iframe (152 px tall, `border-radius: 12px`, `allow="autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; fullscreen; picture-in-picture"`, `utm_source=oembed`), `title`, `thumbnail_url` 300×300; discovery via `<link type="application/json+oembed">` on every open.spotify.com page [S10 FULL, S11]. Suno has **no** oEmbed and no embed endpoint — third parties fall back to iframing the whole app or scraping the audio URL [S12 secondary]; Suno's own share model is "Private/Link Only" by default with a "Public" toggle that feeds the home page and playlists [S13]. **Design consequence:** an `/oembed` endpoint + `<link rel="alternate" type="application/json+oembed">` on every `/t/<id>` is a differentiator versus Suno today.
6. **schema.org gives the vocabulary but Google gives no music rich result.** `MusicRecording` (properties `byArtist`, `duration`, `inAlbum`, `inPlaylist`, `isrcCode`, `musicBy`, `recordingOf`) and `MusicPlaylist` (`numTracks`, `track`) exist [S14 FULL]; Google's structured-data guidance requires that "structured data matches the visible text on the page" [S05] and lists no music-specific rich result (absence measured against S05 only — `[single-source official]`). **Design consequence:** JSON-LD stays (entity understanding for AI Overviews/AI Mode via query fan-out [S05]) but the visible HTML must carry the same facts.

# Framing and falsifiers

Interfaces grow a product when three loops exist: (a) an *artefact loop* — every unit of use leaves a public, machine-readable object others can find (track page, playlist page, embed, credential badge); (b) a *distribution loop* — those objects are carried by other people's surfaces (chat previews, embeds in blogs, playlist inclusion, AI-engine citations); (c) a *return loop* — retention levers (push, adaptive playlists, streaks) bring the sharer back. Each loop below is graded by the strongest evidence class available: RCT/causal econometrics > large-N observational > first-party contract > adoption observation. What would falsify the recommendation is listed in the scope plan.

## F1 — Public track pages and SEO structure (U-f1)

- Google's own rule for AI features: a page must be indexed and snippet-eligible; "no additional technical requirements"; content must be "available in textual form"; "structured data matches the visible text"; AI Overviews/AI Mode use "query fan-out" across subtopics and surface "a wider and more diverse set of helpful links" [S05 FULL]. - **Application:** `/t/<id>` renders, server-side, the prompt text, title, duration, generation date, model/version, Content Credential status, BPM/key where computed, and a plain `<audio>` fallback; the JSON-LD `MusicRecording` mirrors exactly those visible facts (Google's matching rule). `/playlist/<id>` pages carry `MusicPlaylist` with `numTracks` and `track` [S14]. Sitemap grows from the current 23 `/t` URLs to every public track and playlist automatically.

## F2 — oEmbed / OG / Twitter cards / embeds (U-f2)

## Five-part academic records

### S01 — Aral & Walker 2011, Management Science [FULL]

1. **Problem (authors' framing):** firms want to create word-of-mouth peer influence by designing viral features into products, but "econometric identification of peer influence is non-trivial" because homophily and correlated unobservables confound observational estimates [§1–2].
2. **Method:** randomized field experiment on Facebook, 44 days; a movie-rating application with three arms — baseline (viral features disabled), passive-broadcast (automatic notifications to friends), active-personalised (referrals/invites added); 9,687 adopters (405 / 4,600 / 4,682) with 1.4 M distinct peers; outcome = hazard of peer adoption in the local network; variance-corrected stratified proportional hazards (Table 4, Models 1–4) with clickstream decomposition (Table 5) [§3, §4.1, §5.1].
3. **Numbers:** +246% peer adoption (passive) over baseline; +98% additional for active-personalised (344% total); 70,140 viral messages → 992 peer adoptions, 682 in direct response; per additional invite +6% adoption rate vs +2% per notification; adoption rate 0.1% per invitation vs 0.01% per notification, with notifications ≈ 70,000 vs invitations 160 (NIM summary of the same study); comparison: banner CTR 0.10% (2008), paid-search conversion 0.02% [lines 101, 182–215, 509–513].
4. **Limitations:** stated — one application, one platform (Facebook 2010), <2% of users alter privacy defaults (assumption about network visibility), effects measured within local networks over 44 days; analyst — a 15-year-old platform context; modern feed ranking suppresses passive broadcasts far more than 2010 Facebook did, so the *ratio* may differ even if the *ordering* holds; music-listening artefacts are not movie ratings.
5. **Application here:** design the automatic artefact (public track page + preview) as the primary growth mechanism and the personalised invite as the higher-conversion secondary; instrument both separately (per-message conversion) so the ordering can be re-tested on our own traffic.

### S02 — Aguiar & Waldfogel 2018 (NBER w24713) / 2021 (J. Ind. Econ.) [FULL]

1. **Problem:** with streaming, "creators may now be dependent on platform decisions about which songs and artists to promote"; do Spotify's own playlists causally raise streams and drive discovery of new artists? [abstract, §1]
2. **Method:** four strategies — (i) before/after addition to and removal from the five most-followed Spotify-owned global playlists using daily top-200 streams (regression-discontinuity logic, song popularity assumed smooth) [§4.1]; (ii) songs just on vs just off algorithmic Global Top 50 [§4.2]; (iii) cross-country New Music Friday inclusion with song fixed effects across 26 countries [§5]; (iv) IV using home bias in NMF rank differentials. Data: Spotify daily top-200 by country (2017), Spotontrack playlist entries/exits, ISRC for origin and vintage.
3. **Numbers:** as in F6 — +3.346 (0.28) / −2.757 (0.09) normalised daily streams; 74.4-day mean spell; 19.4 M streams; US$116,397–162,956; 259,531 daily streams = 10% of mechanical max; other lists 15–22%; RapCaviar low-end US$60,265, Viva Latino high-end US$424,265; NMF #1 → ~85% chart appearance [lines 229–278, 309–313].
4. **Limitations:** stated — assumes symmetric linear add/remove effects; only songs entering once; payments per stream taken from press (US$6–8.4/1,000); analyst — 2016–2017 data; Spotify's list mechanics have changed; our catalogue (33 public tracks) is orders of magnitude smaller, so the *mechanism* transfers, not the magnitudes.
5. **Application here:** make platform-curated playlists a public, indexable surface and treat inclusion as the product's own promotion lever; report inclusion effects to creators as a retention artefact.

### S03 — Aggarwal et al., GEO, KDD 2024 (arXiv 2311.09735v2) [FULL]

1. **Problem:** generative engines synthesise answers from multiple sources, and "content creators have little to no control over when and how their content is displayed"; traditional SEO does not transfer [§1].
2. **Method:** black-box optimisation of a source's text with nine methods (Authoritative, Statistics Addition, Keyword Stuffing, Cite Sources, Quotation Addition, Easy-to-Understand, Fluency Optimization, Unique Words, Technical Terms); GEO-bench of 10,000 queries from MS MARCO, ORCAS-1, Natural Questions and others; a two-step engine (top-5 Google sources → LLM answer); visibility metrics Position-Adjusted Word Count and Subjective Impression (LLM-judged) [§2–3, lines 76–108].
3. **Numbers:** Table 1 and Table 2 values as in F9; up to +40% visibility overall; up to +37% on Perplexity.ai; fluency/readability +15–30%; keyword stuffing no improvement [lines 27, 136–161].
4. **Limitations:** stated (§9) — simulated engine with top-5 retrieval; LLM-judged impression metric; domain-dependent effects; analyst — 2023–2024 engines; effects on music pages (short, structured content) untested.
5. **Application here:** the `/t` page text template (quote + statistics + cited provenance) and the copy discipline for playlist and profile pages.

### S04 — Chen et al., "Generative Engine Optimization: How to Dominate AI Search", arXiv 2509.08919 (Sept 2025) [FULL]

1. **Problem:** "no published studies regarding whether traditional SEO techniques are relevant and effective for AI search results"; how do AI engines source information versus Google? [§1]
2. **Method:** taxonomy of AI-asked questions from five Reddit communities (top 1,000 hot + 1,000 new posts each); 1,000 consumer ranking prompts (10 categories × 100) run in the U.S. and Canada against Google and web-enabled GPT (plus Perplexity sonar-pro, Gemini, Claude in the benchmark); domains de-duplicated per answer; overlap measured as Coverage@k and Jaccard; source types classified Brand / Earned / Social [§3–4, lines 89, 353–427].
3. **Numbers:** as in F9 — Earned 72.7–92.1% in AI search vs 31.8–54.1% in Google; Social ≈ 0% vs 10.9–23.1%; top-5 overlap 15–32%; Pew: AI summaries on ~18% of queries, clicks 8% vs 15% [lines 63, 445–451].
4. **Limitations:** stated — consumer-product verticals, two regions, snapshot of 2025 engines; analyst — preprint, not yet peer-reviewed at time of reading; music discovery queries were not among the tested categories.
5. **Application here:** earned-media strategy (verifier page, citable research, third-party embeds) weighted above brand-page optimisation for AI-engine visibility.

# Contradictions, corrections, retractions, uncertainty, and gaps

- **Contradiction G1 — llms.txt lift.** Crawlmind [S09] reports "2–4× citation rate uplift" for sites shipping `/llms-full.txt` from *customer interviews*, and "measurable lift … within ~14 days" from its own server logs, while SE Ranking (~300k domains, XGBoost + statistical model: zero effect, model improves when the variable is removed) [S06, S07], Ahrefs (97% never fetched) [S07], MaxAEO (2,400 domains, 8 engines, no lift) [S08] and a 500 M-visit crawler sample (408 requests) [S16] find no effect. Resolution: three independent large-N nulls outweigh one vendor's interview sample; recommendation = ship cheaply for agents, do not budget it as GEO. Preserved, not averaged.
- **Contradiction G2 — Spotify oEmbed path.** GitHub issue #1479 shows `https://open.spotify.com/embed/oembed?url=` returning 404 while `https://open.spotify.com/oembed?url=` works, with Spotify stating a fix "to handle both paths" (Q G1 card). The current reference documents only `/oembed` [S10]. Resolution: use `/oembed`.
- **Uncertainty:** OG field names not re-read from the Open Graph protocol page; Suno's lack of oEmbed is asserted by one secondary; the crawler-rendering generalisation is secondary; magnitudes from S01/S02 are 2010/2017 platform contexts.
- **Gaps (5):** Spotify Canvas/Wrapped first-party evidence; ASO measured evidence; retention levers (push/adaptive playlists/streaks) in this file; Udio; SoundCloud/Bandcamp. Also not done: live inspection of our own `/t/<id>` HTML and a live oEmbed call.

# Synthesis — adopt / build / avoid

- **ADOPT:** Spotify's oEmbed field set and discovery pattern as the embed contract [S10, S11]; Google's "textual form + matching structured data" rule as the page-content law [S05]; schema.org `MusicRecording` / `MusicPlaylist` / `Person` as the entity vocabulary [S14]; GEO content shape — quotation, statistics, citation — for every public page [S03].
- **BUILD:** (1) `/oembed` endpoint + `/embed/t/<id>` compact player with `<link rel="alternate" type="application/json+oembed">` on every public page; (2) automatic public artefacts for every generated track and playlist (the 246% lever) with an opt-out, plus a secondary personalised invite/credit path (the 6%-per-message lever) [S01]; (3) editorial + algorithmic public playlist pages and inclusion notifications [S02]; (4) `/u/<handle>` profile pages; (5) a public `/credentials` verifier page and other earned-citable surfaces, because AI engines cite Earned ≥ 3× Brand [S04]; (6) `/llms.txt` + `/llms-full.txt` generated from the sitemap (1-day item) [S05–S09]; (7) sitemap auto-growth from 23 URLs to all public tracks/playlists/profiles.
- **AVOID:** relying on iframes/embeds as the only carrier of a fact [S05, S15]; keyword-stuffed titles/descriptions (no GE lift) [S03]; a share button as the *only* distribution path (active features are used ~440× less often than passive ones in the RCT: 160 vs ~70,000 messages) [S01]; treating llms.txt as SEO/GEO [S05–S08].

# Committed recommendation and alternatives / tradeoffs / falsifiers

**Recommendation:** the rebuilt surfaces carry a **"public artefact by default" growth spine** — every generated track, playlist and profile is a server-rendered, oEmbed-enabled, schema-annotated page with quotable text and a Content Credentials badge; the Flutter share sheet shares that URL; playlists and inclusion notices are first-class; the personalised invite/credit path is secondary and separately instrumented. This is the interface translation of the two causal results (S01, S02) and the two GEO measurements (S03, S04), and it is exactly what Suno lacks today (no oEmbed, Link-Only default) [S12, S13]. Alternative: an app-first, private-by-default model (Suno's) — lower moderation and IP exposure, but it forfeits the artefact loop the evidence favours. Falsifiers: a replication showing passive artefacts underperform on 2026 feeds; Google/OpenAI documenting llms.txt as a citation input; Spotify deprecating oEmbed.

## Five-part academic records — extension (S17, S18, S19)

**S17 — Hybrid GAT + MLP churn model on WSDM-KKBox.** (1) Problem, authors' framing (line 12): "Most existing churn prediction methods fail to integrate relational dependencies among users or address the limitations of static, tabular models in imbalanced datasets." (2) Method: merge `transactions_v2`, user logs and demographics into one user-level table; engineer static, temporal and behavioural features; build a user-similarity graph on shared traits (line 42, the authors call it a "synthetic user similarity graph"); dual-path Graph Attention Network + MLP (line 148–154); weighted binary cross-entropy with w₁ = 10 for churn, w₀ = 1 (line 212) instead of resampling; decision threshold 0.89 chosen on validation accuracy (line 256). (3) Numbers (line 256): test accuracy 95.87 %, AUC 0.9609; Not-Churn precision/recall/F1 = 0.98/0.98/0.98 on 176,726 users; Churn precision 0.76, recall 0.79; churn base rate ≈ 9 % (line 46, 206). (4) Limitations — stated (line 342): single dataset (KKBox) limits transfer; imbalance handled only by weighted loss. Analyst: the graph is constructed from shared attributes, not from real social edges, so the "relational" gain is a clustering effect; threshold tuned on accuracy over a 91/9 split inflates the headline accuracy; no online deployment. (5) PMP application: PMP has the same three tables in kind (session logs, plan/limit transactions, profile) and a 12/h–40/day free tier whose exhaustion is a natural churn-risk event; a churn score computed server-side can drive which retention surface the rebuilt Home shows (resume-queue card vs. new-prompt card). The interface implication is a *state*, not a feature: "returning at-risk listener" is one of the designed Home states.

**S18 — Deezer Automatic Playlist Continuation, framework + online A/B test.** (1) Problem (line 24, 30–34): extend user-created playlists while preserving musical character, at catalogue scale (tens of millions of candidates) with real-time inference. (2) Method: Represent-Then-Aggregate (RTA) framework — learn song embeddings, aggregate seed-playlist representation, score all candidates; six RTA variants incl. MF-Transformer; offline evaluation on the Million Playlist Dataset with Precision, Recall, R-Precision, NDCG, Clicks, Popularity, Coverage and inference time (line 199–205, 285); online A/B in April 2022 on Deezer, models trained on 25 million private playlists, candidate pool 2 million songs (line 251–253). (3) Numbers: offline, SKNN 27.66 % NDCG vs MF-CNN 26.83 % vs MF-GRU 28.22 % (line 239); Transformer row Precision 5.31 ± 0.09, Recall 40.46 ± 0.47, R-Precision 22.29 ± 0.27, NDCG 29.21 ± 0.37 (line 285); online, p99 inference 12 ms on an AMD EPYC 7402P with ten threads (line 257); **users exposed to MF-Transformer added 70 % more recommended songs to their playlists than the reference cohort** (line 259; absolute rates and cohort sizes withheld by Deezer). (4) Limitations — stated (line 231–235): baselines dropped for scalability; single-stage models only; exact rates confidential. Analyst: relative lift without base rate cannot be converted to retention; playlists are user-authored on Deezer whereas PMP tracks are prompt-generated, so "continuation" maps to prompt-lineage rather than catalogue recall. (5) PMP application: the ONE listening queue is PMP's playlist; an "extend this queue in the same spirit" affordance (the live page already carries a prompt ending "continue in the same spirit as the previous track", S22) is the APC loop; the measured 70 % add-rate lift is the precedent for making continuation a first-class Queue control rather than a buried option.

**S19 — Review of AI automation and subscription response in music streaming.** (1) Problem (line 22–26): how AI automation shapes subscription responses across recommendation, pricing, retention, marketing and support. (2) Method: narrative literature review (no PRISMA flow or database list found in the capture — grep for "systematic|PRISMA|Scopus" returned only cited-work titles at lines 189 and 363); themes: recommendation (§2), hyper-personalisation, dynamic pricing (§3.1), churn prediction (§3.x, line 82–88), targeted marketing (§4), social integration (line 100–108), chatbots (line 114–118), sentiment analysis (line 126). (3) Numbers: relayed figures only — "80 % of consumers are more likely to purchase … from a brand that provides personalized experiences" (line 48, citing Carole et al. 2024); "62 % of consumers prefer to communicate with a customer service AI agent" (line 96, citing Santiago 2023). Both are second-hand and are marked `[relayed]`. (4) Limitations — stated: none found in the capture as a dedicated section. Analyst: a narrative review with marketing-survey numbers; useful as a map of retention levers (early churn-signal detection → incentives, line 84; social sharing as acquisition, line 100–106), not as measured evidence. (5) PMP application: confirms the lever inventory the rebuilt surfaces must expose — early-signal detection (server), personalised re-entry (Home state), social share paths (F3), and in-product support — but every quantitative claim must be re-sourced before it enters a design decision.
